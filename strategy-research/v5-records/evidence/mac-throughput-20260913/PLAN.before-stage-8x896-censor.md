# Mac FULL-batch throughput search

This is an engineering-only benchmark of the current corrected worker package on the persistent Mac worktree. It always runs the same eight 920M development seeds and the same FULL geometry (450 event/control pairs, 900 trades per artifact). It never runs held-out seeds, invokes qualification, or changes strategy parameters.

Each invocation runs one fresh, uniquely named full batch. Stage receipts bind the new JAR, source fingerprint, plan, profile, baseline/controls/experiment, and every embedded support-data receipt. The frozen payload envelope stays intact; experiments vary outer JVM process concurrency and actual `-Xmx`, with `ActiveProcessorCount=1` and G1 held fixed. The 4-worker, 1024 MiB batch completed all eight jobs in 2,094.071 seconds. The first 8-worker, 1024 MiB attempt was censored after 2,515.780 seconds with 0/8 jobs complete, just beyond 1.20× the completed reference; it is only a slower-than-reference lower bound, never a completed makespan. Given that result, test 8 workers at 896 MiB next to see whether the smaller heap changes full-batch throughput, before falling back to 6 workers. If 8×896 is slower by more than 5% or is censored, run fresh matched batches of the incumbent configuration and that candidate before confirming a loss or stopping the 8-worker direction. If that direction loses or fails, try 6 workers at the best successful heap. A censored stage, even when it crosses the reference-based time bound, remains a lower bound and cannot be the incumbent. If a heap OOMs, retry that worker count in a new directory at the next larger heap (1152, then 1280 MiB); never retry a known OOM heap blindly or retry at a smaller heap. After choosing the fastest complete worker count, test downward by 128 MiB steps from the lowest successful heap, stopping at 512 MiB. These bounded observations identify only the best tested complete configuration; they do not establish a global or absolute minimum.

Only a complete eight-artifact batch that passes the packaged strict validator, preserves the raw artifact bytes, and passes the frozen independent replay can be compared or retained as an incumbent. The new artifacts must match the reference per seed on exact metrics and the event, control, and combined equity curves. Reference provenance differences may be normalized only at `/row/raw_evaluator_result/physical_input_sha256`, `/row/raw_evaluator_result/corrected_input_binding/physical_input_sha256`, and `/row/raw_evaluator_result/physical_source_producer/plan_sha256`; normalization is recorded after strict validation and never rewrites artifacts.

Whole-batch makespan decides throughput. A first observed batch slowdown beyond 5% triggers fresh matched incumbent/candidate batches; a confirmed slowdown stops that direction. A 3.5% per-worker runtime penalty is acceptable when the higher worker count improves full-batch makespan; this allowance never accepts a slower full batch. An optional time censor may stop a candidate at 1.20 times the incumbent's complete makespan; it is recorded only as a censored slower-than-reference lower bound and is never a completed result. A censored, failed, OOM, partial, or unvalidated stage cannot become best. Any detected clock jump or sleep discrepancy invalidates timing comparison.

Per-worker RSS/CPU use one shared process-table sample per second; disk usage is sampled every 30 seconds, and pressure/swap/VM snapshots every 10 seconds. Full-GC count, pause time, pause fraction, swap growth, and memory pressure are diagnostics. None is an automatic failure. Hard stops remain worker OOM/nonzero exit, invalid output, the 90-minute worker or six-hour batch watchdog, a 64 GiB stage-directory ceiling, or less than 10 GiB filesystem free space. No stage is launched automatically by this harness; the operator reviews and archives each completed or failed directory before selecting the next configuration.
